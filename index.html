Italian constructions: dovere, volere, potere · necessity verbs & adjectives
Construction Family 1 · Responsibility
An IMPOSER / VOLITIONER / ENABLER / PERMITTER construes a RESPONSIBLE AGENT as obliged, willing, or able to perform an ACTION, optionally directed at an AFFECTED OBJECT or resulting in a GOAL STATE.
Modal: DOVERE

Deontic Obligation Construction

dovere
Semantic function
An IMPOSER (external or self-imposed) construes a RESPONSIBLE AGENT as obliged to perform an ACTION, optionally directed at an AFFECTED OBJECT or toward a GOAL STATE. The IMPOSER may coincide with the agent (self-obligation) or be a distinct entity assigning responsibility to another.

Modal: VOLERE

Volitional Construal Construction

volere
Semantic function
A VOLITIONER expresses a will that a RESPONSIBLE AGENT (co-referential or distinct) performs an ACTION. The RESPONSIBLE AGENT may be self-directed, projected onto another party, or deagentivized. When VOLITIONER and RESPONSIBLE AGENT are distinct, the construction additionally assigns responsibility to the embedded agent.

Modal: POTERE

Ability / Permission Construction

potere
Semantic function
An ENABLER (intrinsic capacity of the agent) or PERMITTER (external authority or circumstance) construes a RESPONSIBLE AGENT as able or permitted to perform an ACTION. The source of enablement may be self-generated or externally granted; it may be asserted, negated, counterfactual, or retrospective. When ability is attributed to a second or third person by the speaker, the construction additionally functions as responsibility assignment.

Note on person

The person and number of the modal are diagnostically relevant: 1st-person forms tend to construe the speaker/group as both ENABLER and AGENT (self-enabling); 2nd-person forms shift the enablement or permission to the addressee, often acquiring accusatory or hortatory force; 3rd-person forms maximally dissociate ENABLER/PERMITTER from the named AGENT. Counterfactual and retrospective forms (conditional, pluperfect subjunctive) systematically implicate unrealized or missed capacity, triggering accountability readings regardless of person.

Construction Family 2 · Impersonality
An IMPERSONAL NECESSITY PREDICATE (verb or adjective) construes an ACTION as objectively necessary or important, with the RESPONSIBLE AGENT either suppressed entirely, embedded in a subordinate clause, or deagentivized through the impersonal si.
Necessity verbs & adjectives

Impersonal Necessity Construction

bisognare · essere necessario / importante
Semantic function
An IMPERSONAL NECESSITY PREDICATE presents an ACTION as objectively required or important; the RESPONSIBLE AGENT is either fully suppressed (non-referential subject), embedded as an explicit or implicit subject in a subordinate clause, or generalized through impersonal si. No IMPOSER is lexically identified: the necessity is construed as context- or norm-derived.

Key contrast with the Responsibility family: the Impersonality constructions never lexicalize an IMPOSER. The source of necessity is always external and norm-derived, making these constructions pragmatically stronger for universalizing obligation while rhetorically deflecting personal accountability.
